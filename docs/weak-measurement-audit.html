FOLLOW-UP NOTE / A JOINT STRESS CONDITIONSIMULATED DATA

Row-bootstrap inference
under stress.

Resampling can account for uncertainty that a two-step estimator overlooks. Whether it controls false positives is a separate empirical question.

This experiment tests one jointly lower-loading, smaller-sample condition (λ = 0.50, N = 385). The two coefficients being compared are equal by construction. Every rejection is therefore a false positive in this simulated condition.

It follows the original inference audit, using fresh surveys. Both loading and sample size differ from the earlier positive-pair bootstrap study; a cross-study difference cannot isolate a loading effect. Rates are neither pooled nor paired with the earlier studies.

04 / SCOPE & REPLICATION

One deliberate stress test.
One bounded inference.

These are synthetic Gaussian surveys from a specified measurement and structural model. This condition cannot establish performance under other loadings, sample sizes, nonnormal data, misspecification, or real workplace measurement.

Neither a lower rejection rate nor a successful numerical fit makes the procedure universally reliable. Interpret rejection frequency alongside its uncertainty, failures, and the exact condition tested.
